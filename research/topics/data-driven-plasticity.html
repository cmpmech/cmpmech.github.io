Data-driven multiscale plasticity

Concurrent multiscale (FE2) simulations of plasticity are often bottlenecked by expensive fine-scale solves. A neural-network surrogate structured to mirror classical return-mapping updates can replace traditional FFT homogenization at the meso-scale, reducing per-analysis costs from hours to seconds with speed-ups up to tens of thousands.

The complete workflow in our methodology using a neural-network surrogate covers training data generation from incremental homogenization, compact dataset construction, embedding material symmetries, and deploying the trained network as a user-defined material subroutine (UMAT) in standard finite-element solvers. By eliminating the storage of extensive microscale state variables, this approach opens the door to high-fidelity discretizations across general dimensionalities and symmetry assumptions.
