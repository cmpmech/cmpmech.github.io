Laser beam shaping

Laser beam shaping designs the laser's intensity profile to control melt pools and temperatures in powder bed fusion.

The beam shape can be optimized for the melt pool geometry, or inverse-designed to follow a prescribed temperature field. Bead-on-plate experiments show how well thermal simulations hold across beam shapes.

Letting the beam shape change along the scan path goes one step further. In a multi-track scan, heat builds up from track to track, and a static beam's melt pool grows. Dynamic beam shaping optimizes the intensity profile in space and time, with adjoint-based gradients, so that every track keeps the melt pool width and depth of the first one. For a Gaussian beam, the melt pool would otherwise grow by 175 % in width and 35 % in depth over ten tracks; the optimized dynamic beam keeps it nearly constant.

The same works for non-Gaussian beams. A chair-shaped beam, inverse-designed for a rectangular melt track, loses that shape as heat builds up; its optimized dynamic version keeps every track rectangular.
