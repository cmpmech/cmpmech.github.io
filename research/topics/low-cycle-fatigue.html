Low-cycle fatigue analysis

Predicting low-cycle fatigue failure in ductile materials requires capturing complex cyclic interactions between plasticity and material degradation. A plastic damage model featuring Chaboche-type mixed isotropic–kinematic hardening augmented by a spectral stress-split damage formulation accurately reproduces stiffness degradation and peak stress drop in high-strength aluminum under extreme plastic strains.

The model incorporates a nonlinear activation function to smoothly distinguish between tensile and compressive states, alongside distinct damage indices for the volumetric and deviatoric stress components. Calibrated against experimental data, this constitutive framework enables robust finite-element simulations of structural components subjected to severe cyclic fatigue loading without numerical instabilities during crack propagation.
